# 29. Monitor Quality Scorecard

Coverage is easy to grow and easy to fake. Quality is what decides whether
on-call is sustainable, so it gets its own score — published per team, reviewed
monthly, and gating CI.

Generated by [`tools/monitor_scorecard.py`](../tools/monitor_scorecard.py) into
`generated/scorecard.{json,md}`.

---

## The model

Every monitor is scored out of 100. The weights are the platform's beliefs,
made numeric:

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures | Zero when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Actionability** | 25 | Runbook, automation, and enough context to act at 3am | no runbook, no workflow, no explanatory note |
| **Ownership** | 20 | A registered team **and** a routing path that terminates somewhere | the notification profile defines no route for this priority — the alert goes nowhere |
| **Detection** | 15 | Predictive where the signal is behavioural; justified where fixed | a fixed threshold on a behavioural signal with no rationale |
| **SLO linkage** | 15 | Attached to a real objective, with live telemetry | `slo_id` does not resolve |
| **Cardinality** | 10 | Bounded grouping, collapse key on wide fanouts | a banned identity key in `group_by` |
| **Paging** | 10 | Pages only where policy permits | pages from staging, or a P2 symptom pages |
| **Metadata** | 5 | Complete catalog entry | a required field missing |

**Grades:** A ≥ 90 · B ≥ 80 · C ≥ 70 · D ≥ 60 · F below.

**CI gate:** fleet average ≥ 85 and zero F. Both are enforced on every pull
request (stage 14), so quality cannot erode one merge at a time.

### Reading that number honestly

A fleet average of 96.5 is **not** evidence that the scorecard is demanding.
The catalog was authored against exactly the policy the scorecard checks, so it
grades well by construction. The score has two real jobs, and neither is
self-congratulation:

1. **Regression detection.** The score can only go down, and CI fails when it
   does. A future pull request that adds an archetype without a runbook, or
   routes a priority to a profile that has no route for it, is caught by a
   number rather than by review attention.

2. **Grading self-service monitors.** Catalog archetypes are written by the
   platform team under review. Manifests are written by product teams under
   deadline pressure, and that is where quality actually drifts. Every
   self-service monitor is scored on the same scale, published in the same
   table, and visible to the same monthly review.

The remaining 3.5 points across the estate are almost entirely
`actionability`: archetypes with neither a design note nor a threshold
rationale, which the scorecard flags as *"a responder reading this alert at 3am
has nothing to orient on"*. That is a real, small, fixable backlog.

## Runtime dimensions

The static score runs without credentials so it can gate every PR. With
`--live`, four runtime signals fold in — these are the ones that decide whether
on-call is actually sustainable:

| Signal | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Pages per team per week** | < 5 | The burnout metric. Nothing else on this page matters if this number is wrong |
| **Actionability rate** | > 70% | Share of pages that led to a change. Below this, people start acknowledging without reading |
| **Auto-resolved without action** | < 20% | Alerts that recovered on their own. Higher means the threshold or window is wrong |
| **Flap rate** | 0 monitors | Anything transitioning > 4× per hour is muted and ticketed automatically |

Published on the **Alert Quality** dashboard
(`stacks/foundation/dashboards/alert-quality.json`), which also tracks the
detection mix (predictive vs fixed), the noisiest monitors, unmanaged monitor
count, and the managed-monitor budget.

## The review cycle

| Cadence | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Every PR** | CI | Fleet average ≥ 85, zero F, or the build fails |
| **Weekdays** | governance workflow | Coverage report + scorecard published to the run summary |
| **Monthly** | observability-platform + domain owners | Alert Quality board: pages per team, top 20 noisiest monitors, actionability rate |
| **Quarterly** | domain owners | Archetype catalog review; exception re-approval sweep; RBAC access review |

### The retirement rule

**A monitor that stays below grade C for two review cycles is retuned or
deleted.** Keeping a bad monitor is not neutral — it trains people to ignore
alerts, which degrades every good monitor in the estate.

---

## What the scorecard cannot see

Stated plainly, because a quality metric that hides its blind spots is worse
than none:

- **Whether the detection is correct.** A monitor can score 100 and watch the
  wrong metric. Only incident review catches that.
- **Whether a runbook is any good.** It checks that one exists and has all ten
  mandatory sections. 906 sections across the estate are still marked
  `TODO(owner)` — tracked as a backlog by
  `tools/generate_runbooks.py --report`, and reported per domain.
- **Whether the SLO objective is right.** It checks the SLO resolves and its
  telemetry is live, not that 99.9% is the correct number for that service.
